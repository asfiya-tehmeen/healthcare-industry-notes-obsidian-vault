# Drug Discovery & Preclinical

## Simple explanation
Scientists find the "lock" (a disease-linked protein/gene = **target**) and search for a "key" (molecule) that fits. Then test the key on cells and animals for safety before any human gets it.

## Steps
1. **Target identification/validation** – genetics, literature, omics.
2. **Hit finding** – high-throughput screening of millions of compounds; or AI/virtual screening.
3. **Lead optimization** – tweak chemistry for potency, selectivity, ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion).
4. **Preclinical safety** – toxicology in two species, under **GLP**.
5. **IND-enabling package** → file with FDA.

## Data types & tools
- Chemistry: SMILES strings, molecular fingerprints (RDKit).
- Bioassays: IC50 curves, plate readers.
- Omics: genomics (VCF/FASTQ), transcriptomics, proteomics.
- LIMS (Lab Information Management System), ELN (electronic lab notebook, e.g. Benchling).
- Public DBs: ChEMBL, PubChem, DrugBank, UniProt, Open Targets.

## Why data people care
Messy scientific data, lots of ontologies, FAIR data principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable). Roles: bioinformatics engineer, scientific data engineer.
