# Inspections, Audits, Quality Systems

## Simple explanation
An **audit** is a check by your company or customer; an **inspection** is by a regulator. Both ask: "show me the evidence you follow your own procedures and the rules."

## Types
- Internal audits, supplier/vendor audits, sponsor audits of CROs/sites
- Regulator inspections: pre-approval (PAI), routine GMP, GCP site inspection, PV inspection, for-cause
- FDA outcomes: **Form 483** (observations) → **Warning Letter** → consent decree/import alert
- EU: GMP certificate/non-compliance statement

## How an inspection runs
Opening meeting → document & system review ("show me the audit trail for batch X") → interviews → daily wrap-ups → close-out → written response with **CAPA** within 15 working days (FDA 483).

## Your role as data/compliance person
- Retrieve evidence quickly (audit trails, access lists, validation packages, training records)
- Explain data flow diagrams
- Never alter records during inspection; "backroom" = document control

## Most common findings
1. Data integrity (no audit trail, shared logins)
2. Inadequate investigations / CAPA
3. Unvalidated systems/spreadsheets
4. Poor change control
5. Training gaps
6. Failure to follow SOPs

## Quality KPIs (analytics ideas)
Deviation cycle time, CAPA overdue %, right-first-time batch %, OOS (out-of-spec) rate, audit finding trends, training compliance %.
